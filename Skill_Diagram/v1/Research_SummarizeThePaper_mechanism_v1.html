Research_SummarizeThePaper — from a paper to a faithful summary

One paper, or a small set, read in full and summarized only as far as the text supports.

1Get the paperyou
A DOI, URL, PDF or supplied text.
2Read the primary paper
Methods, results, figures or tables, discussion. Abstract only: say so prominently and limit the conclusions.
3Extract the evidence
Question, study type, population, controls, methods; each finding traced to a result, figure or table, with units and denominators.
4Weigh the limitations
The authors' stated limitations plus any that affect the claims; your own assessment marked as an inference.
5Separate the layers
What the data show, what the authors propose, and what remains unknown, including conflicting results.
6Write for the request
Question, approach, findings, significance, limitations, with depth matched to the user's purpose and claims attributed to the paper.
Never
  • Infer details from the title or abstract alone
  • Turn association into causation, or relative enrichment into absolute abundance
  • Invent bibliographic details, quotations, statistics or links
When access is incomplete
  • Describe the gap instead of filling it from memory
  • Inspect supplementary data before relying on it, and cite it separately
  • Favor the version of record
